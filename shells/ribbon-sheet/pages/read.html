Four colours, one board

The board, by Jenny Henderson Studio, is as plain as a palette gets: four cards, each with its name, its hex value, and its RGB and CMYK equivalents. Two of them are dark, two are light, and one of the light ones is warm enough to read as a sunset.1

A board like this is a mood, not a system. It says nothing about what a disabled label looks like, which colour a link is, or how a chart tells six series apart. Getting from one to the other is the work.

Roles, not colours

An RT Coat theme never paints a component. It moves roles: the ground, the card, the text, the action, the mark. Every component asks for a role, so a theme that sets about a hundred of them reaches every button, badge and chart without naming one.

For Coast the mapping came down to one table:

Where each colour of the board lands
ColourBy dayAt night
Onyxtext, the ribbon, dark surfacesthe ground and the cards
Wheatthe ground and the wellstext
Oceanicthe action, with white inkthe mark, lifted
Nectarinethe mark, with Onyx inkthe action, with Onyx ink

The numbers decide

Taste picks the mapping; contrast checks it. Every pair of ink and fill the library uses is measured in both modes, and a theme ships only when none falls under its floor: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and edges.

Contrast of the pairs that carry text
Ink on fillRatioVerdict
Onyx on Wheat17.0:1body text
White on Oceanic11.7:1action
Onyx on Nectarine11.1:1mark
Nectarine on Wheat1.5:1never as text

One pair is out

Nectarine on Wheat measures 1.5:1. By day Nectarine is therefore only ever a filled shape with dark ink on it, never a thin line or a word on the page.

A mood board says how a thing should feel. The contrast table says whether anyone can read it.

Night is not inverted day

Swapping light for dark is the obvious first move, and it fails in a specific way: Oceanic, the colour that did all the work by day, all but vanishes on Onyx. So at night the two accents trade places. Nectarine becomes the action, and Oceanic, lifted to a sea glass, becomes the mark and the first chart series.

Try it

Coast ships with RT Coat like every other theme. In this Workspace, open Settings in the ribbon, or press CtrlK and type theme. To make it a Workspace's default:

  1. Create the Workspace with it: rt init --theme coast my-app.
  2. Then check its contrast with the rt-ui skill every Workspace carries:
Role
A named colour job, such as --primary or --bg-elevated, that components ask for.
Floor
The least contrast a pair may have: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and edges.

Any shell, any theme

A theme names no component and no shell, so Coast works on Ribbon & Sheet, the Showcase and Canvas alike.

Questions

Why not use the board's own typeface?
A theme may only name faces the library already hosts, so no page ever asks a third party for a font. A wide display face can join the library first, and Coast can then name it.
Can a theme belong to one shell only?
Yes: a shell may carry its own themes in shells/<name>/themes/. They are offered with that shell and nowhere else.
What happens to the charts?
They read six chart roles. Coast sets them from the board's colours and their tints, with a separate set at night.
